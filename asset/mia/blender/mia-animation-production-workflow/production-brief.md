# Mia production brief — execution

Primary design authority: the preserved master v22 and confirmed 16-expression sheet in `inventory.json`. These are illustrated design references with pose/camera variation, not metrically consistent orthographic scans.

- Adult feminine proportions; narrow, tired gray eyes, defined dark upper lid, restrained under-eye tone, small dry mouth. The face should remain recognizable in front/profile/three-quarter and expression combinations.
- Ash/graphite long disordered waves, substantial rear volume, half-up attachment and one anatomical-left braid. Preserve the successful flow where possible; evaluate scalp clearance, neck/shoulder room and replaceability separately.
- Structured gray crop with raised hem and separate light inner band, oversized charcoal cardigan with low rear hem, loose cargo trousers and anatomical-right slate panel, high-top shoes.
- Strap from anatomical-right shoulder to anatomical-left rear hip. Bag, flexible strap and rigid hardware have separate contact/deformation responsibilities. Avoid duplicate braids, bags, straps and pins.
- Build the whole silhouette early, including rough garment and hair masses. Detail face/body, hair, garments and accessories in the documented order while retaining feedback between related parts.
- Compare source appearance and neutral clay/wire. Painted or toon shading is part of likeness, but cannot establish that hidden geometry, depth or deformation structure exists.

Stage 0 makes the reuse decision. Pixal, donor-derived studies and existing hair/body work are evidence and candidate data, without mandatory adoption or blanket rejection. A generated high-detail surface may serve as a visual/high-resolution source while production topology is reconstructed only where necessary.

Stage 1 is a non-destructive 3D blockout. Current source UV/color may guide it; stable production topology, final texture transfer, facial/body rigging, stress feedback, secondary motion and calibrated 30fps clips follow their own checks.
